Life After Insolvency — You Can Still Get a Car

Going through a bankruptcy or a consumer proposal is one of the hardest things a person can face. It often feels like a fresh start — but also like a closed door. Many banks will not lend to you right away. But getting a car loan is still very possible.

AutoBanker specializes in helping people in exactly your situation. We work with lenders across Canada who specifically approve borrowers who have gone through financial hardship. You are not alone, and you are not stuck.

📌 Important Distinction: Bankruptcy is a legal process where most debts are wiped out. A Consumer Proposal is an agreement to pay back a portion of your debt over time. Both appear on your credit report, but they affect your loan eligibility differently.

Bankruptcy vs. Consumer Proposal: What's the Difference?

Both options help people who cannot pay their debts. But they are not the same thing. Here is a simple breakdown:

Personal Bankruptcy

  • Most or all of your debts are cancelled.
  • You may have to give up some assets.
  • A first bankruptcy stays on your credit report for 6 years after discharge.
  • You are "discharged" (finished) in as little as 9 months.
  • Harder to get financing while still bankrupt — but possible after discharge.

Consumer Proposal

  • You pay back a portion of what you owe.
  • You keep your assets (car, home, etc.).
  • Stays on your credit report for 3 years after completion.
  • Takes up to 5 years to complete payments.
  • You can often get a car loan while still in a proposal.

How Long Does It Stay on Your Credit Report?

This is one of the most common questions we hear. Here is the timeline you need to know:

First-Time Bankruptcy
6 Years
After your discharge date. Equifax and TransUnion both follow this rule.
Second Bankruptcy
14 Years
A second filing has a much longer impact on your credit file.
Consumer Proposal
3 Years
After you complete all your payments. This is shorter than bankruptcy.
Individual Accounts in Proposal
6 Years
Each account included in a proposal has its own 6-year reporting window.

Can I Get a Car Loan During a Consumer Proposal?

Yes — in many cases, you can. Getting a car loan while in a consumer proposal is more common than you might think. Lenders who specialize in subprime financing know that a consumer proposal is a sign of responsibility. You are taking steps to pay your creditors back. That matters.

⚠️ One Important Thing to Know: If you are in a consumer proposal, you may need permission from your Licensed Insolvency Trustee (LIT) before taking on new debt above a certain amount. Always check with your trustee first.

If you have been discharged from bankruptcy, you can start applying for a car loan right away. You do not need to wait. The sooner you start making on-time payments, the faster your credit score recovers.

What Lenders Look For After Insolvency

  • Stable income: A steady job or consistent income is the most important factor. Lenders want to see you can afford the payment.
  • Time since discharge or filing: The longer it has been since your bankruptcy or proposal started, the better. But even day-one approvals are possible.
  • A down payment (optional but helpful): Even $500 to $1,000 can improve your chances and lower your rate.
  • A reliable vehicle choice: Choosing a practical, lower-cost vehicle shows lenders you are responsible.
