Why Big Banks Reject Self-Employed, Contractor & Gig Drivers

Over 18% of workers in Toronto, Brampton, Mississauga, and across the GTA earn their living outside traditional T4 payroll—working as independent contractors, skilled tradespeople, small business owners, realtors, truckers, or rideshare and delivery drivers on Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, Instacart, and SkipTheDishes.

When you apply for a car loan at a traditional bank branch, automated underwriting asks for two years of personal T4 slips and Notices of Assessment (NOAs) showing high net taxable income. Because smart self-employed Canadians legally write off business expenses, fuel, vehicle depreciation, and supplies to lower their taxable income, their Line 15000 net income looks artificially low on paper—triggering an unfair bank decline.

💡 How AutoBanker's 90-Day Bank Statement Program Works: Instead of judging your tax write-offs, our specialized Canadian Stated-Income and Bank-Statement lenders look at your last 90 days of actual gross bank deposits. As long as your business or personal bank statements show at least $1,800 to $2,200/month in gross deposits, we can verify your income in hours—no T4 slips required.

Frequently Asked Questions

Direct underwriting answers from AutoBanker's Ontario finance desk.

Can I get a car loan in Ontario if I drive for Uber, Lyft, or DoorDash?

Yes. AutoBanker works with specialized Canadian auto lenders that accept Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, Instacart, and SkipTheDishes earnings. By reviewing your last 90 days of bank statements showing regular platform deposits of at least $1,800/month (combined with any other income), we secure fast approvals.

What if my Notice of Assessment (NOA) shows low income because of business write-offs?

That is the #1 reason self-employed drivers use AutoBanker instead of a regular bank. Our bank-statement programs calculate your gross monthly deposits over the past 90 days rather than penalizing you for legal business deductions on your tax return.

Can I finance a car in my corporation's name or personal name?

Both options are available. Most self-employed drivers in Ontario choose to finance in their personal name (or co-sign with their corporation) because it unlocks faster approvals, $0 down options, and builds personal Equifax and TransUnion credit while still allowing business mileage deductions.

What if I am paid in cash or e-Transfers as a contractor?

As long as you deposit your cash or e-Transfer earnings into a Canadian bank account so they appear on your 90-day bank statement history, our non-prime and near-prime lenders count them as verifiable income.
