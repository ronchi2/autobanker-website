What is Negative Equity ("Being Upside-Down") on a Car Loan?

Negative equity happens when the remaining payoff balance on your current auto loan is higher than your car's current wholesale trade-in value. For example, if you owe $18,000 on your existing car loan, and dealers appraise your vehicle at $14,000, you have $4,000 in negative equity (often called being "upside-down" or "underwater").

Thousands of Ontario drivers find themselves in negative equity because they bought a vehicle during peak 2022–2024 used-car prices, took a high-interest subprime loan at a traditional dealership, or racked up high highway mileage commuting across the GTA. When your family outgrows your car—or your current vehicle starts needing expensive out-of-warranty repairs—you do not have to pay that $4,000 gap out of pocket.

⚡ How Ontario Lenders Roll Over Negative Equity (Up to 140%–160% LTV): Canadian prime and non-prime auto lenders use a metric called Loan-to-Value (LTV). While a standard loan covers 100% of a vehicle's book value, AutoBanker's lending partners allow up to 135% to 160% LTV on select late-model vehicles—letting us legally pay off 100% of your old car loan and roll the difference into a newer, safer vehicle with $0 cash out of pocket.

Frequently Asked Questions

Direct underwriting answers from AutoBanker's Ontario finance desk.

How much negative equity can be rolled into a new car loan in Ontario?

Depending on your credit tier, gross monthly income, and the book value of the new vehicle, Canadian auto lenders typically allow between $3,000 and $10,000+ of negative equity to be absorbed (up to 140%–160% of the new vehicle's Canadian Black Book wholesale value).

Can I trade in an upside-down car if I have bad credit?

Yes. As long as you have made consecutive on-time payments on your current car loan for at least 10 to 12 months and earn $1,800+/month, subprime and near-prime lenders reward your auto payment history by allowing you to trade up.

How does the Ontario 13% HST trade-in tax credit work?

In Ontario, when you trade in a vehicle toward another vehicle in the same transaction, the trade-in amount is deducted from the purchase price before calculating the 13% HST. A $20,000 trade-in immediately saves you $2,600 in tax.

Do I need cash down to trade in a car with negative equity?

In most cases, no cash down is required when we pair your 13% HST tax credit with a vehicle from our GTA inventory that carries strong Canadian Black Book value.
